I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Racah Institute of Physics, The Hebrew University of Jerusalem (HUJI). I received my Ph.D. from HUJI, where I studied galaxy evolution using cosmological simulations.

My current research focuses on fast radio bursts (FRBs) as probes of diffuse ionized gas in the cosmic web, the sheets, filaments, and circumgalactic medium that contain the majority of baryons in the Universe. Using analytic methods, I model how FRB signals (scattering, scintillation, and dispersion measure) are shaped by foreground plasma, translating observables into testable predictions for forthcoming surveys. My future directions include machine-learning-based joint inference of cosmic-web gas properties from these FRB observables, structure formation physics across scales (from the cosmic web to star-forming filaments in molecular clouds), and the circumburst environments of FRB sources.


A common thread in my current and planned work is applying similar analytic tools across very different scales, so that insights from one regime can inform another.
